# First follow-up: make the v1.1 study defensible

Status: **readiness plan, not a frozen executable protocol**. The primary question remains Revision 3's question: can adaptive task acquisition identify a useful fixed profile with less total research cost than uniform acquisition? It does not estimate conditional routing benefit.

## 1. Produce a field and trace inventory

Inventory eligible low-risk digital tasks by project lineage, task family, terminal outcome, profile and verifier. For each required field, record available, absent, inferred or measured, with a concrete source and quality flag:

| Evidence | Needed for |
|---|---|
| Immutable task/input/environment/lineage | Repeated comparisons and leakage control |
| Requested and effective runtime/model/harness/effort/context/tool settings | Establishing that the profile intervention ran |
| Independent verdict and verifier scope/version | Trustworthy success labels |
| READY, execution, verification and committed-PASS timestamps | Capped wall-clock TTVS, including overlapping work |
| All attempts, timeouts, errors, pauses and unknown invocation outcomes | Complete denominators and recovery cost |
| Usage, price basis, cache status, wait/quota observations and missingness | Separating resource savings from accounting changes |
| Replay eligibility and complete three-profile coverage | Determining which offline comparisons are identifiable |

Do not fill unobserved provider internals with estimates labeled measured. A partial historical panel cannot support arbitrary counterfactual profile outcomes. Report usable complete panels separately from incomplete traces.

## 2. Qualify profiles and the verifier

Define the three compatible profiles and a frozen target cohort. Bind all materially different settings and verify supported adapter behavior. Include scope-matched correct, incomplete, subtly incorrect, corrupted and adversarial artifacts in verifier qualification. Report incorrect artifacts among all accepted artifacts, and accepted artifacts among all independently confirmed incorrect artifacts, as distinct rates with appropriate uncertainty and independent labels.

Decide whether the pilot can distinguish candidates: an all-success easy cohort may expose cost differences but not justify lower-success-risk or escalation claims. Include genuinely difficult eligible tasks if the claim requires them. Qualification and dry-run checks have their own budgets and evidence class.

## 3. Freeze a complete budget

For acquisition arm `a`, let `n_a` be acquired development tasks, `r_a` paired repetitions, and `N_h`, `r_h` common-holdout tasks and repetitions. With all three profiles on each task:

`profile_trials = 3 × sum_a(n_a × r_a) + 3 × N_h × r_h + qualification_trials + declared_extra_trials`.

Use this as an accounting identity, not a recommendation for sample size. Count costs of selectors, proposal calls, verification, restore/retry and review separately. Specify whether a shared completed panel is reused in an offline acquisition simulation; such reuse is not two independently executed live arms.

Revision 3's illustrative 20–24 development profile-trial cap needs an exact interpretation. Complete batches allow at most six tasks at cap 20 or eight at cap 24 before repeats. The 18-trial illustrative holdout means six tasks × three profiles once. The uniform acquisition comparator cannot silently be omitted from total cost. Choose final counts only after inventory-based precision and correlation analysis.

## 4. Specify the two stages without mixing their claims

**Offline acquisition replay:** use an eligible complete historical panel to compare frozen adaptive and uniform acquisition rules. Preserve selection histories, inclusion probabilities and costs. This measures acquisition decisions on that panel, not a new live runtime benefit. Incomplete support stays a limitation.

**Prospective pilot, when ready and authorized:** use matched task pools, predeclared execution order and all-profile pairing. Candidate identities and analysis freeze before a common untouched holdout. Model selection and calibration roles must be separate if an independence-based calibration claim is used. A tiny pilot may support feasibility or reveal missing data while remaining inconclusive about success non-inferiority.

## 5. Predeclare the decision

Choose the success margin, effect size, confidence procedure, independent grouping unit, cost basis, missingness rules, horizon, positivity/ESS gates and stop rules before dependent observations. Keep every allocated non-PASS chain in the denominator. Use project-aware paired analysis; repeats within a project do not create new independent projects.

The adaptive rule proceeds only if success constraints and a meaningful total-cost/TTVS improvement meet the frozen gates. Otherwise retain uniform acquisition or the governed baseline. Do not rescue a failing rule by changing the final holdout, widening its cohort after seeing results, or counting only successful cases.

## 6. Reviewable output of the readiness work

The next concrete artifact should contain an inventory table, three immutable profile manifests, verifier qualification design, split/lineage map, full trial-and-cost budget, analysis specification, and a list of unresolved fields. Trace values, owners, numerical margins and execution authority are currently unresolved. No new provider calls, training or physical tests were started by this research review.
