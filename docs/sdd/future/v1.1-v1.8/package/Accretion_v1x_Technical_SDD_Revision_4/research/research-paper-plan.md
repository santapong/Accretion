# A focused first paper from the v1.x program

**Artifact:** research proposal outline. No Accretion experimental findings or novelty claim are asserted.

**Working title:** Cost-Aware Profile Selection for Governed Coding Agents: A Preregistered Feasibility Study.

Eight software releases are a development program, not automatically eight scientific contributions or one coherent paper. The first paper should answer one question that v1.1 can evaluate. Later recovery, memory, harness adaptation and embodiment work can become separate studies if their evidence supports a contribution.

## Central question

Under a fixed total research budget, can adaptive task acquisition select a useful fixed compute profile more efficiently than a uniform rule while retaining independently verified success on unseen project lineages?

The unit of choice is one of three fixed profiles. The study does not claim a general online router, automatic verifier design, model-training benefit or physical safety. Selection regret, capped TTVS and full research cost are complementary outcomes, not interchangeable definitions of success.

## Proposed argument

1. **Problem:** cheaper calls or fewer evaluations can fail to reduce the cost of achieving a verified result. Profile settings and evaluator behavior may also differ from their requested configuration.
2. **Evidence-backed motivation:** task acquisition and harness research motivate economical evaluation, while the reviewed papers expose confounded harness effects, weak baseline choices and selection/calibration pitfalls.
3. **Proposed method:** three immutable compatible profiles, effective-setting receipts, scope-qualified verification, paired acquisition, a uniform comparator, explicit data roles and complete resource accounting.
4. **Planned evaluation:** frozen acquisition rules tested on an eligible complete historical panel, followed—only when ready—by a separately authorized prospective pilot with a common untouched holdout.
5. **Possible outcome:** adaptive acquisition may win, tie or lose. A transparent negative finding that a simple uniform rule is sufficient would still inform the project. The paper's claim must follow the data.

## Manuscript structure and evidence needed

| Section | Content | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | One practical bottleneck and narrow question | Trace inventory and relevant primary literature |
| Related work | Acquisition, harness/compute interaction, trustworthy evaluation | Verified source cards; include contradictory and negative evidence |
| Method | Candidate identity, acquisition rule, verifier, data access and authority boundaries | Frozen specification and actual implementation artifacts |
| Setup | Eligible population, lineage splits, repetitions, budgets and analysis | Preregistered protocol with resolved numerical choices |
| Results | Success, TTVS, regret, cost and missingness | Preserved local result bundle; currently absent |
| Mechanism analysis | Activation, difficult-task behavior, cache/accounting and selection stability | Predeclared controls rather than favorable post-hoc slices |
| Limitations | Population, sample size, provider drift and generalization | Observed limits and unsuccessful cohorts |
| Reproducibility | Data permissions, versions, splits, scripts and receipts | A shareable, redacted replication bundle |

Write methods and related work before results exist. Write the abstract's results sentence only after the analysis is complete. Assess novelty against the precise evaluated contribution at submission time; a governance wrapper or SDD alone does not establish scientific novelty.

The installed research-to-design skill provides a writing guide. Within this package, the current numbered SDDs, document 24 and the shared protocol govern the study; this outline provides a manuscript structure and contains no experimental findings.
